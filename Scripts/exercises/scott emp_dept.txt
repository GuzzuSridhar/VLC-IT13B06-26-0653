# Oracle SQL Exercises – Traditional SCOTT Schema

These exercises use the traditional Oracle `SCOTT` schema tables: `EMP` and `DEPT`. They are arranged from beginner to advanced and cover `SELECT`, `WHERE`, sorting, functions, joins, subqueries, and set operators.

## Section 1: Basic SELECT Statements

1. Display all records from the `EMP` table.
2. Display only employee name, job, and salary.
3. Display all department records from the `DEPT` table.
4. Display employee names and their hire dates.
5. Display employee names and salaries, sorted by salary in ascending order.
6. Display employee names and salaries, sorted by salary in descending order.
7. Display distinct job titles from the `EMP` table.
8. Display employee names and annual salaries.
9. Display employee names and commission values.
10. Display employee names and their employee numbers.

## Section 2: WHERE Clause and Operators

1. Display employees whose salary is greater than 2000.
2. Display employees earning less than 1500.
3. Display employees whose salary is between 1000 and 3000.
4. Display employees working as `MANAGER`.
5. Display employees who work as either `CLERK` or `ANALYST`.
6. Display employees who do not work as `SALESMAN`.
7. Display employees who belong to department 10.
8. Display employees whose commission is NULL.
9. Display employees whose commission is NOT NULL.
10. Display employees whose names begin with the letter `S`.
11. Display employees whose names end with `N`.
12. Display employees whose names contain the letter `A`.
13. Display employees whose salary is not equal to 3000.
14. Display employees who earn more than 1500 and belong to department 10.
15. Display employees who earn more than 2500 or work as `SALESMAN`.

## Section 3: Sorting and Filtering

1. Display employees ordered by department number and salary.
2. Display employees ordered by hire date, from earliest to latest.
3. Display employees ordered alphabetically by name.
4. Display employees ordered by job in ascending order and salary in descending order.
5. Display the top 5 highest-paid employees using `ROWNUM`.
6. Display the employee with the lowest salary using a subquery.
7. Display employees hired before 1982.
8. Display employees hired during the year 1981.
9. Display employees whose salary is between 1000 and 2500, sorted by salary descending.
10. Display the first 3 employees from the result ordered by hire date.

## Section 4: Single-Row Functions

1. Display employee names in uppercase.
2. Display employee names in lowercase.
3. Display employee names with the first letter capitalized.
4. Display each employee's name and the number of characters in the name.
5. Display the first three characters of every employee name.
6. Display employee names and salaries rounded to the nearest hundred.
7. Display employee names and their annual salaries using `ROUND`.
8. Display employee hire dates in the format `DD-MON-YYYY`.
9. Display employee names and their salaries after adding a 10% increase.
10. Display employee names and the number of months they have worked, using `MONTHS_BETWEEN`.
11. Display employee names and the last day of their hire month.
12. Display employee names and their salaries padded with leading zeros to a width of 6 characters.
13. Display employee names and salaries formatted using `TO_CHAR`.
14. Display employee names and commission values, replacing NULL commissions with zero using `NVL`.
15. Display employee names and salary grades using a `CASE` expression: below 1000, 1000–2000, and above 2000.

## Section 5: Aggregate Functions and GROUP BY

1. Find the total number of employees.
2. Find the maximum salary in the company.
3. Find the minimum salary in the company.
4. Calculate the average salary of all employees.
5. Calculate the total salary paid to all employees.
6. Count the number of employees in each department.
7. Find the average salary for each job.
8. Find the maximum salary in each department.
9. Find the total salary paid for each job.
10. Display departments having more than 3 employees.
11. Display jobs whose average salary is greater than 2000.
12. Find the difference between the highest and lowest salaries.
13. Count how many employees receive commission.
14. Find the average commission of employees who receive commission.
15. Display departments where the total salary exceeds 5000.

## Section 6: Joins Between EMP and DEPT

1. Display employee names and their department names.
2. Display employee names, job titles, and department locations.
3. Display employees working in the `DALLAS` location.
4. Display employees working in the `NEW YORK` location.
5. Display employee names and department names, sorted by department name.
6. Display employees earning more than 2000 along with their department names.
7. Display each department name and the employees working in it.
8. Display all departments, including departments with no employees.
9. Display employee names and their managers' names using a self-join.
10. Display employees whose managers work in department 10.
11. Display employees who work in the same department as `SMITH`.
12. Display department names and the total number of employees in each department, including departments with zero employees.

## Section 7: Subqueries

1. Display employees earning more than the average salary.
2. Display the employee earning the highest salary.
3. Display employees earning less than the average salary.
4. Display employees who earn more than `JONES`.
5. Display employees working in the same department as `KING`.
6. Display employees who have the same job as `SMITH`.
7. Display the second-highest salary.
8. Display employees earning the second-highest salary.
9. Display employees earning more than every employee in department 30.
10. Display employees earning more than at least one employee in department 30.
11. Display departments that have at least one employee.
12. Display departments that have no employees.
13. Display employees earning more than their department's average salary.
14. Display employees whose salary is greater than the average salary of their job.
15. Display employees who earn the highest salary in their respective departments.

## Section 8: Set Operators

1. Display the job titles found in department 10 or department 20 using `UNION`.
2. Display job titles common to departments 10 and 20 using `INTERSECT`.
3. Display job titles in department 10 but not in department 20 using `MINUS`.
4. Combine employee names and department names into a single result using `UNION ALL`, ensuring compatible column types.
5. Display employee numbers who are managers or who work in department 10, using `UNION`.
6. Display employee numbers who are managers and work in department 10, using `INTERSECT`.

## Section 9: Advanced SELECT Challenges

1. Display each employee's name, salary, and salary rank using `RANK`.
2. Display each employee's name and dense salary rank using `DENSE_RANK`.
3. Display employees earning more than their manager.
4. Display each department's highest-paid employee.
5. Display the top 3 highest-paid employees in each department.
6. Display employees hired before their managers.
7. Display employees who earn more than the average salary of the entire company but work in department 30.
8. Display each employee's salary as a percentage of the total salary expenditure.
9. Display department-wise employee counts and the overall employee count in one result.
10. Display employee names, salaries, and a running total of salaries ordered by employee number.

Suggested practice order: Sections 1–3 first, then functions and aggregation, followed by joins, subqueries, set operators, and advanced queries.

Use the standard SCOTT sample data, where `EMP` contains employee information and `DEPT` contains department information. These exercises require only `SELECT` statements; no table creation or data modification is necessary.
