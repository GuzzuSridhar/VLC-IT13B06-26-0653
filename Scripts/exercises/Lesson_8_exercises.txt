## Oracle SQL Subquery Exercises

Use the Oracle HR schema (EMPLOYEES, DEPARTMENTS, JOBS, and LOCATIONS tables) to solve the following exercises. Write SQL queries only.

### 1. Subquery: Types, Syntax, and Guidelines

1. Display the employees who earn more than the average salary of all employees.
2. Display the employee details of the person who earns the highest salary.
3. Find the employee who earns the second-highest salary using a subquery.
4. Display employees who work in the same department as the employee named 'Abel'.
5. Display the names of employees who earn more than the employee 'Taylor'.
6. Find the department name in which the employee 'King' works.
7. Display employees whose salary is greater than the average salary of employees in department 50.

### 2. Single-Row Subqueries

8. Display employees whose salary is greater than the salary of employee 'Lex De Haan'.
9. Find employees who earn the same salary as employee 'Karen Colmenares'.
10. Display employees who joined the company before the employee 'Steven King'.
11. Find employees who work in the same department as employee 'Neena Kochhar'.
12. Display the employee details of the person who earns the minimum salary.
13. Find employees whose commission percentage is equal to the commission percentage of employee 'John Russell'.
14. Display employees whose salary is greater than the salary of employee 'David Austin'.

### 3. Multiple-Row Subqueries

15. Display employees who work in departments located in the United States.
16. Find employees whose department IDs match the department IDs of employees working under manager ID 100.
17. Display employees whose salaries match any salary earned by employees in department 60.
18. Find employees who earn more than any employee working in department 50.
19. Find employees who earn more than all employees working in department 50.
20. Display employees who work in departments that have at least one employee earning more than 10,000.
21. Find employees who do not work in any department where employee salaries exceed 15,000.
22. Display department names that contain employees earning more than the overall average salary.

### 4. Multiple-Column Subqueries

23. Display employees whose (department_id, salary) combination matches that of an employee working in department 50.
24. Find employees whose (manager_id, department_id) combination matches that of employee ID 103.
25. Display employees whose (job_id, salary) combination matches that of an employee in department 60.
26. Find employees whose (department_id, job_id) combination matches the combination of employee ID 100.
27. Display employees whose department and salary match the department and salary of any employee reporting to manager ID 100.
28. Find employees whose (job_id, department_id) combination matches that of employees earning more than 12,000.

### 5. NULL Values in a Subquery

29. Display employees who do not appear in the list of employees reporting to manager ID 100, using NOT IN.
30. Write a query to demonstrate how a NULL value returned by a subquery affects the result of NOT IN.
31. Display employees whose employee IDs are not present in a subquery result that includes a NULL value.
32. Rewrite a NOT IN query using NOT EXISTS and compare the results when the subquery can return NULL.
33. Find employees who have no colleagues in their department, using a correlated subquery.
34. Display departments that have no employees using NOT EXISTS.
35. Write a query to identify employees who have no manager and explain how NULL affects the comparison.
